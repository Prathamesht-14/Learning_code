Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, also known as Shivaji Bhonsale, was a prominent Indian ruler and a member of the Bhonsle dynasty. He was born around February 19, 1630, and passed away on April 3, 1680. Shivaji is celebrated for his significant contributions to Indian history, particularly for founding the Maratha Empire, which played a crucial role in challenging Mughal authority and expanding territories. Shivaji’s early life was marked by his mother Jijabai’s influence, who was deeply religious and ambitious. He served the King of Bijapur during his youth and later rebelled against the Mughal Empire and other neighboring states. His kingdom’s security was based on religious tolerance and the integration of various social groups, including Brahmans, Marathas, and Prabhus. In 1674, Shivaji enthroned himself as an independent sovereign and ruled his domain via a cabinet of eight ministers. He is known for his strategic military campaigns and alliances, as well as his daring escape from Agra in 1666 after being imprisoned by Aurangzeb. This escape solidified his reputation as a cunning and resilient leader. Shivaji’s navy was also a significant part of his administration. He recognized the importance of maritime trade and constructed a navy to protect his territory and ports.
